## 1. The video in one line

A blind three-way tasting of Maker's Mark, Maker's Mark 101 and Maker's Mark Cask Strength. It scores each one on taste and on UK cost per 25ml measure, then gives a clear "who should buy which" verdict that points viewers to the written review.

- **Working title:** Maker's Mark vs 101 vs Cask Strength: Blind Test (UK Prices)
- **Target length:** 10 to 12 minutes
- **Extras:** 3 Shorts cut from the same shoot

## 3. Angle and promise

Same distillery, same wheated recipe, three strengths. The video answers one question for UK drinkers: **is the 101 the smart middle buy, or should you save with the standard bottle or go all in on Cask Strength?**

Why this angle works:

1. It uses the format that already wins on YouTube (blind, head to head).
2. It adds the UK detail nobody else covers: pound prices, cost per 25ml measure, UK retailers.
3. It sends viewers to the written review, which carries the Amazon UK link.

## 10. Fact check before filming

Some claims in the article need confirming before they go on camera. A spoken mistake is much harder to fix than a line of text.

| # | Claim in the article | Problem | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Aging: Minimum 5-7 years" | Maker's 101 carries no age statement. | Say "no age statement" unless an official source gives an age. |
| 2 | "Unfiltered presentation" (repeated across the article) | No source in the article supports it. | Confirm with the brand's page or a press release. Drop it from the script if you can't. |
| 3 | "Introducing Maker's 46 about ten years ago" | Maker's 46 launched in 2010, about 16 years ago. | Say "in 2010" and fix the article too. |
| 4 | Travel retail exclusive in 2018, domestic release in 2020 | Other reviewers repeat this, but the article's only source is a retailer page. | Find a brand or press source. Until then, say "it started as a travel retail release". |
| 5 | Standard Maker's at 90 proof | That is the US strength. Bottles sold outside the US may be 40% ABV instead of 45%. | Read the ABV off the bottle you film. If it says 40%, the jump to 50.5% becomes a key point. |
| 6 | "Premium bourbons in the £40-60 price range" | The 101 itself is listed at £71.99. | Use the real price band on camera. |
| 7 | Wheat % of 18% for Weller and 20% for Old Fitzgerald | Neither brand publishes its mash bill. | Leave these off screen, or label them "reported". |
| 8 | Amazon price of £71.99, 4.6 stars from 61 ratings | A snapshot that will go out of date. | Say "about £70-75 for a litre at the time of filming" and keep the live link in the description. |
| 9 | Presenter experience: Kentucky festival in 2021, 200+ whiskeys, certified bourbon steward, 15 distilleries | Personal claims. | Only say these on camera if they are true for the person presenting. |

## 11. UK rules to follow

- **Affiliate disclosure:** say it out loud near the price segment and put it at the top of the description, not lower down. UK advertising rules (ASA/CAP) treat affiliate links as marketing.
- **Alcohol content:** the CAP Code alcohol rules apply to marketing. Nothing with strong appeal to under-18s, no link between drinking and social or sexual success, and nothing that encourages heavy drinking. Anyone shown drinking must be over 25 and look it. Keep pours small and the tone adult.
- **YouTube settings:** set "Made for kids" to No. Tick "Includes paid promotion" if a brand or retailer supplied any of the bottles.
